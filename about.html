A note before we begin

This is not a résumé. It is a chronicle — the record of one man's refusal to stay still. Read in order, it's a life that moves the way real lives actually move: not in a straight line upward, but in loops. Sales to teaching. Teaching to entrepreneurship. Entrepreneurship to entertainment. Entertainment to construction. Construction to hospitality. And hospitality, at last, back to the earth. Each loop closes carrying something forward. Nothing here is decoration — it's inheritance.

Chapter One

Where It Began

Born 15 May 1980 · Mumbai

Vishal Singh was born on 15 May 1980 and raised in a lower-middle-class joint family in Mumbai — the son of Mr. Ramnayan Singh, an M.Sc. Gold Medalist from Mumbai University who became a Professor and later a Builder, and Mrs. Usha Singh, an Economics Topper from Kanpur University and, in time, a Share Investor. Around him grew two sisters equally marked by academic distinction: Kavita, a Psychology Topper from Mumbai University who now teaches psychology in Switzerland, and Pallavi, a Mumbai University graduate who works today as a tutor.

It was a household built on achievement — and it set a quiet, formidable bar. Vishal's own schooling at Bangur Nagar Vidhya Bhavan closed with recognition as the school's best student across academics, extracurriculars, and sport. He went on to graduate in Economics from St. Andrews College of Arts as college topper, and later topped six consecutive GNIIT software programs, earning his Microsoft Certified Professional credential along the way.

But alongside academics, another identity was forming just as powerfully: Vishal was becoming an athlete of national standing — selected for the 1999 Asian training camp to represent India in the 2000 Olympics in the 400m and 800m, recognised as the Most Decorated Athlete under the government's Project 2000, and a National Champion in Heptathlon and Pentathlon.

"A household of gold medals and toppers does not merely educate a child — it dares them. Somewhere between his father's blackboard and his own hurdles track, Vishal learned the lesson that would define the next three decades: excellence is not a title you inherit, it is a target you keep having to hit again."
Chapter Two — April 1999

The Challenge That Started It All

RSG Infotech · Sales Executive · Apr 1999 – Apr 2001

The true beginning of Vishal's professional life wasn't a career plan — it was a moment of family pride directed, for the first time, somewhere else.

"This happened out of curiosity — for the first time, my parents were found praising my cousin brother for his achievement of bagging an MTNL job, then tagged at ₹15,000 per month. It set a challenge for me to put my abilities to the test, and hence, without being a graduate, I decided to take a walk in the corporate world."

He began at RSG Infotech as a Sales Executive on ₹5,000 a month plus commission, introducing schools and colleges across Mumbai to Apple Macintosh machines. Within a short span his payouts crossed ₹15,000 a month — matching the very benchmark that had sparked the challenge. But the win felt incomplete. Sales quotas were being met; a sense of purpose was not. He kept surfing for opportunities, and landed an interview at Davar's College as a Computer Teacher.

Chapter Three — 2001 to 2002

Building the Builders

Davar's College · Center Administrator, IT Division · Andheri West & Malad West

At Davar's College, Vishal moved from selling machines to shaping minds — organising IT curriculum, increasing enrolment, training new teachers, and designing courseware across two outlets, working alongside Mr. Darabshaw Davar. He built a course catalogue priced so students of every economic background could afford it, not just those who could pay premium-institute rates.

The scaling was easy. What followed was harder — watching students complete their training only to return, asking him for jobs he could not give them. That gap became the seed of his first real venture.

"Knowledge in today's world without monetisation is lost money — so I resigned and began Tech Hunt Developers."
Chapter Four — 2002

The First Fall, and What It Taught

Tech Hunt Developer · Proprietor

Tech Hunt Developer was Vishal's first venture as a founder — enrolling students and training them to launch their own e-commerce businesses, years ahead of India's digital retail boom. It was an idea with real integrity, and one that ran headlong into an unwinnable fight.

"Least did I know then that locking horns with Amazon and Flipkart is a race lost much before we began. While a few of my students are still doing well... I felt the numbers, in comparison to putting India on the world map, were still minuscule."

He closed the venture rather than let it drift into something he no longer believed served its students honestly. He was twenty-two — a rare kind of self-awareness for a founder mid-venture, willing to call an idea premature rather than force it to work. That instinct to walk away from something not yet right would return again and again across his career, each time at real cost, and each time preserving something more valuable: his own standard for what a venture should be.

Chapter Five — 2002 to 2006

Building Better, Not Just Bigger

PcGenesis · Founder & Proprietor

From the ashes of one venture came the discipline for the next. With PcGenesis, Vishal deliberately narrowed his ambition — supplying the means for others' growth rather than promising outcomes he couldn't guarantee. He built a network of sub-dealers, each selling ten units to become authorised HCL resellers.

The scale that followed was extraordinary for an independent operator: 250 HCL PCs sold in a single month across the Mumbai suburbs, and formal recognition as the highest HCL seller in the Maharashtra Mumbai suburbs.

It was also during this period that a registered dealership with Marching Ants — a Bollywood post-production and design firm — opened a door into an industry he knew existed but had never seen the pathway toward. Watching artists he'd only seen on screen sitting and refining their own image planted a curiosity that would soon pull him out of hardware entirely.

Chapter Six — 2002 to 2010

Bollywood, Boardrooms, and a Hard Lesson in Power

Mangal Murti LLC · Business Development Manager

At Mangal Murti LLC, Vishal's role was to build ties and secure investment — connecting Bollywood with foreign direct investment. His network came to include Shree Ashtavinayak Cinevision, RGV, Kumar Mangat, Big Pictures, Ronnie Screwvala, ABCL, Subhash Ghai, and more.

His defining achievement was helping structure a deal between Shri Ashtavinayak Cine Vision and a Dubai-based company. It was a career-making transaction — until it wasn't.

"To my surprise, the moment they shook hands I was pulled out of the equation — without realising that it also happens in India."

It was a formative lesson in how deals, credit, and power move in high-stakes industries — one that didn't deter Vishal from entertainment, but pushed him to build his own platform, where he would answer to no one else's handshake.

Chapter Seven — 2008 to 2014

Building His Own Stage

VRU Entertainments Pvt. Ltd. · Founder & CEO, Events Division

VRU Entertainments was where Vishal took full ownership of the entertainment world he'd only glimpsed before — directing production-house operations across Dubai and India, with hands-on command of budgeting, recce, pre-production, production, and post-production.

The portfolio built over six years reads like a cross-section of India's entertainment landscape at the time: execution of the Videocon Screen Awards and Filmfare Awards, Grasim Mr. India & Miss India, advertising for the Aditya Birla Group, scripting for Satyamev Jayate and Kaun Banega Crorepati, music albums (Kisse Kahun, Aaye Mahi), films (An Last Autograph, Ishq Chupta Nahi, Floating Flowers, Cages), and launch events for Lonely Planet, Nissan Micra, Lee Jeans, and Yakult.

Perhaps the single most notable achievement of this chapter: Vishal directed the production-house operations that introduced Jacqueline Fernandes to the Indian media industry, launching her as the face of Collection G for D Damas — a debut that helped set her on the path to Bollywood stardom.

From the VRU Entertainments years — inside the industry he'd once only watched from a distance.

But beneath the glamour of awards shows and celebrity launches lay the unglamorous truth of running a production business — and Vishal never hides from it.

"However, our monthly bread and butter was from Birthday Parties, Corporate Events, and Catering."

It's this candour — naming both the marquee wins and the humble, reliable revenue that actually kept the lights on — that runs through his entire account of his career.

Chapter Eight — 2014 to 2015

From Stage Sets to Steel and Stone

King's Timber Construction · Founder, Partnership Firm

Entertainment gave way to construction — a venture managing labour contracting and interior-design projects, coordinating on-site workforce, resource allocation, quality control, and timelines. Vishal built homes and furniture using ready-block construction methods, uncommon in the market at the time, delivering structures with minimal delay.

The chapter's centrepiece project was extraordinary in scale and largely unknown outside the trade:

"The most prestigious and unknown project was the making of a 1.4 lakh sq. ft. structure of the Goa Jail."

It stands as a striking data point in Vishal's range — a Bollywood production executive turned builder of one of the largest correctional facilities constructed on the western coast during that period.

Chapter Nine — 2016 to 2021

The Birth of a Brand — Tipsy Monkee

V Hospitality (First Chapter) · Proprietor

In 2016, Vishal brought together two long-held skill sets — catering and event management — to build something Mumbai's nightlife hadn't quite seen before.

"We are proud pioneers of Wednesday midweek party culture, and have successfully launched entertainment giants like Mohit Modanwal and Dakx Band as products of the Tipsy Monkee brand — which later started becoming a craze among Mumbai's party hub, and now you see many brands with names inspired by TIPSY or MONKEE around party places."

Parallel to it, and continuing well beyond, Vishal owned and hosted Tipsy Monkee itself — a role spanning ten full years (2015–2025) of daily hospitality leadership, patron relations, and guest retention. Without quite planning it, he had become one of Mumbai's recognised architects of midweek nightlife culture — proof that a brand built on a single original idea, defended with consistency, could outlast the trends around it.

Chapter Ten — 2022 to 2025

When the Licence Was Pulled — and the Comeback

V Services · Proprietor

If one chapter defines Vishal's resilience above all others, it's this one. When the licences for food and liquor at his venues were revoked, most operators would have closed the doors. Vishal instead rebuilt the entire business model, single-handedly, around what remained.

"The challenge presented itself when the licences for food and liquor were revoked — however, God always leaves a window open for seekers, and thus our team of six stood up to the challenge by booking gross profits of over ₹16 lakh per month through strategies uncommon to most."

The insight was sharp and specific. A Maharashtra Government policy restricted liquor service to guests above 21, locking an entire, energetic segment of younger Mumbai out of the city's clubs. Vishal built a business precisely for them — ₹500 entry for a five-hour dance platform, no food, no liquor, purely music and movement. It drew 150+ dancers daily, sustained entirely on entry revenue, with a team of just six people.

It's one thing to survive a regulatory shock. It's another to read that shock correctly enough to build a thriving new model inside it, at speed, without outside capital or a large team.

Chapter Eleven — 2025 to Present

V Hospitality Today

V Hospitality · Founder & Managing Director

V Hospitality today stands as the refined, institutional expression of everything built and learned across V Services and the Tipsy Monkee era — hospitality and recurring event operations running at genuine scale, including high-capacity dance events operating four days a week, built on a model generating approximately ₹16 lakh in monthly collections.

Chapter Twelve — 2021 to Present

Back to the Earth — V Agro and The Pandora City

V Agro · Proprietor

Even while V Services and V Hospitality demanded daily attention, Vishal was quietly building a parallel life — hands in the soil, learning cultivation, ploughing, and farming from the ground up.

"Every time a difficult situation stood mocking me, I would go back to Mother Nature for guidance... Now, after all the achievements, experiences, and defeats, I could see that it was a chosen pathway by a higher power, preparing me for a project that could bring all areas of experience together."

That project is The Pandora City — the deliberate convergence point of every chapter that came before it: fitness and athletics into a human-scale Adventure Park; agricultural learning into a full-grown organic Fruit and Vegetable Farm; veterinary knowledge, gained through sheltering abandoned dogs, into a Goat, Fish, Turkey, Duck, Dog, Cow & Buffalo Farm and Milk Centre; years of hosting guests into a Weekend Getaway and Jungle/Tent Living platform; and event management skills pulled together into an Eco-Vibrant Luminous Garden experience — plus an annual musical fest named Forever Fyre, envisioned to include the nation's biggest New Year's celebration.

Alongside the land runs a parallel body of written work: THE DIVINE CODE, a simplified guide for numerology aspirants, and AN AUTOBIOGRAPHY OF GOD — Volume I, I AM GOD — which Vishal describes simply as a way to become one with God.

"And what would be more impressive: each of these units of commercially promising money-making machines is individually capable of being self-sufficient and giving lucrative returns to its believers and supporters."

The Thread That Ties It Together

Read end to end, this career resists the tidy, single-lane story most executive profiles try to tell. He has sold computers and sold out dance floors. He has taught curriculum and built a Goa jail. He has launched a Bollywood face and launched an organic farm. Each pivot, on paper, looks like a departure. Read in his own words, each one is a continuation — the same restless, disciplined, competitive instinct that first showed up on an athletics track and in a family of academic toppers, now applied to whatever room he happened to walk into next.

What emerges is a genuinely rare profile: someone equally credible discussing vendor logistics for a 65-acre agri-tourism build, negotiating an entertainment industry investment, coaching an athlete's conditioning programme, or reading a regulatory setback quickly enough to turn it into a ₹16-lakh-a-month business within the same year.
